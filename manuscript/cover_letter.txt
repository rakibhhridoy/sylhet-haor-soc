To the Editors,
Journal of Environmental Management

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Soil Organic Carbon under Warming, Drying and
Agricultural Intensification: Integrating Field Sampling, Multi-decadal Remote Sensing and
Reanalysis in the Sylhet Haors, Bangladesh," for consideration as a research article in the
Journal of Environmental Management.

Wetlands in South and Southeast Asia are major soil carbon stores under intensifying climatic
and land-use pressure, yet their management is hampered by sparse field data and by
remote-sensing signals that are readily confounded in seasonally flooded systems. Our study
addresses this by integrating five independent lines of evidence for nine haor wetlands of the
Sylhet Basin, Bangladesh -- field soil sampling, multi-decadal Landsat indices, Sentinel-2
land-cover classification with a formal accuracy assessment, ERA5-Land climate reanalysis, and
benchmarking against the global WoSIS soil database -- to reach management-relevant conclusions
that no single data stream could support alone.

We believe the work fits the scope of the Journal of Environmental Management for several
reasons:

1. It delivers actionable management guidance for wetland carbon -- sustaining the seasonal
   flood pulse, returning organic matter through rice management, directing urban expansion
   away from high-carbon cores, and establishing long-term soil/temperature/hydroperiod
   monitoring.

2. It contributes a transferable, confound-aware methodology of direct relevance to
   environmental monitoring: we show that inundated-area imagery alone can misrepresent
   hydrological change (the basin is warming and drying even though its wet-season footprint
   appears stable), and we demonstrate how dry-season compositing and independent reanalysis
   resolve this.

3. Its central soil finding -- that in clay-rich, acidic wetland soils SOC is governed by
   nitrogen and organic-matter supply rather than by clay -- is externally validated against
   approximately 9,700 comparable profiles in the WoSIS database and a space-for-time climate
   analysis, giving the nine-site result broad generalizability.

We have been deliberately transparent about the limitations of the field dataset (nine sites,
a single 2025 sampling, and a secondary 1985 baseline), and we frame the long-term SOC
comparison as suggestive rather than definitive; the manuscript's quantitative conclusions rest
on the statistically supported environmental trends and the large-sample external benchmarking.

This manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by
any other journal. All authors have approved the submission and declare no competing interests.
The datasets and analysis code supporting the study are available in a repository, with external
data sources documented.

We suggest the manuscript may be suitable for editors/reviewers with expertise in wetland
biogeochemistry, land-use/remote-sensing assessment, and soil carbon management.

Thank you for considering our work.
